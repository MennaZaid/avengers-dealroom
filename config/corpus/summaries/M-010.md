# The Mom Test: rules we apply (team summary)

Source: Rob Fitzpatrick, *The Mom Test* (book, all rights reserved): https://www.momtestbook.com
This is our own summary of the method, not text from the book.

Why it matters: people are polite. If you describe your idea and ask whether they like it, most say yes, and
you learn nothing. The Mom Test is a way to ask questions that even someone who wants to please you cannot
answer with a comfortable lie.

Rules the interview designer follows:
1. Talk about the customer's life, not the idea. Don't pitch during the interview.
2. Ask about specific past behaviour ("the last time this happened, what did you do?"), never about the future
   ("would you use...?", "would you pay...?"). Opinions and predictions are not evidence.
3. Ask what they have already tried, what it cost them in money and time, and why it didn't work. Someone who
   has not tried anything probably doesn't care enough.
4. Look for commitment and advancement: time, reputation or money the person gives (a follow-up meeting, an
   introduction, a pre-order). Compliments and "keep me posted" are not commitments.
5. Keep it casual and short; ask for an introduction to the next person to talk to.
6. Write down what was said, in their words, right after the conversation. Separate facts (what happened) from
   your interpretation.

Signals the research critic treats as weak evidence: compliments, hypothetical answers, generic statements
("everyone needs this"), and the founder's own fluff ("they said they'd definitely buy it").

How Venture OS uses it: the interview designer writes past-behaviour questions only and lists questions to
avoid; interview results come only from the founder (typed primary_founder_reported); the Discover gate counts
an interview as problem evidence only if it describes the problem from past behaviour.

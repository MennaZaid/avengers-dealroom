# Beachhead market and bottom-up TAM, Disciplined Entrepreneurship (team summary)

Source: Bill Aulet, *Disciplined Entrepreneurship* (book, all rights reserved):
https://disciplinedentrepreneurship.com. Our own summary of the method, not text from the book.

Beachhead market: pick one narrow first market where you can win, where customers have the money and an urgent
need, where you can reach them with the resources you have, and where winning helps you move into neighbouring
markets. Narrow means customers who buy similar products in similar ways and talk to each other.

Bottom-up TAM (total addressable market) for the beachhead: count the customers, then multiply by what each
would pay per year. TAM = number of target customers x annual revenue per customer. Build the count from
sourced numbers (e.g. registered businesses, internet users in the segment), not from a top-down share of a
big industry figure. Then:
- SAM (serviceable available market): the part you can reach with your channels, language and geography.
- SOM (serviceable obtainable market): the part you could realistically win in the first years.
Give ranges, show the formula and the source of every input; a guessed input is an assumption.

How Venture OS uses it: the market definer picks a beachhead from the brief's target market and computes
TAM/SAM/SOM bottom-up as ranges, with every input sourced or labelled ASSUMPTION.

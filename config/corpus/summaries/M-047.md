# How to Get Startup Ideas, Paul Graham (team summary)

Source: Paul Graham, "How to Get Startup Ideas" (2012): https://www.paulgraham.com/startupideas.html
Essay: all rights reserved. Our own summary; short quotes only, with the link.

Main points: the best ideas come from problems the founders have themselves or see up close, not from
brainstorming "startup ideas". A good idea is something a small number of people want a lot, urgently, rather
than something many people want a little. Beware "made-up" or "sitcom" ideas that sound plausible but that no
one actually needs. Ask who wants this right now, and so much that they will use a crude first version made by
a small team. Look for what is missing, and for why existing alternatives fail these users.

How Venture OS uses it: the opportunity finder prefers alternatives close to the founder's skills and lived
experience; the idea scorer rewards evidence of urgent pain in a small, reachable group over vague broad
appeal; the research critic flags ideas whose only support is that they "sound good".

# Menna & Omar: everything from scratch

## Part 1: The game in 60 seconds (both read this)
- We pretend to be a **VC fund**: investors who give money to young startups in exchange for a **share of the company**.
- We have **$1M**, and 13 startups asked for money. We picked 5.
- When a startup grows, our share becomes worth more. Most startups fail, but **one big winner pays for all the losers**. This is called the **power law**.
- The judges want our **decisions and reasons**, not a description of each startup.

## Part 2: Business words, explained for Menna (the engineer)
| Word | Meaning (engineering analogy) |
|---|---|
| Cheque / ticket | The money we put into one startup. |
| Pre-money valuation | What the company is "worth" before our money comes in. |
| Post-money | Pre-money plus the new money raised. |
| Stake / equity % | Our share of the company = cheque ÷ post-money. For example, $290K ÷ $2.78M = 10.4%. |
| Dilution | Each later funding round issues new shares, so our % shrinks, like adding nodes to a cluster and owning fewer of them. We assume we keep 66% of our stake (data from Carta). |
| ARR | Annual recurring revenue: yearly subscription income. The strongest proof that customers pay. |
| Burn / runway | Burn = money spent per month. Runway = months until the cash runs out (cash ÷ burn). |
| Multiple | Investors value a company at X times its revenue; SaaS is about 4–8x. |
| Base vs downside | The expected scenario vs the bad scenario. |
| 6.2x | We turn $1M into $6.2M in the base case. 0.35x = we get back $350K in the downside. |
| Reserve | Cash we keep for **follow-on**: investing more later in the winners. |
| SAFE / cap | A note that converts into shares later. The "cap" is the maximum valuation it converts at (IGNOT: $3.5M). |
| FRA | Egypt's Financial Regulatory Authority. It licenses fintech. |
| Sandbox | The regulator lets a startup operate under supervision before full licensing. A strong signal. |
| Scorecard / Berkus | Two standard ways to put a price on a startup that has no profits yet. |

## Part 3: The tech, explained for Omar (the business student)
Think of the tool as **a team of very fast junior analysts**:
1. **Reader.** It read every deck, video (converted to text) and Excel model.
2. **Fact-checker.** It took 67 claims founders made (e.g. "150 locations") and checked that the exact words appear in their own materials. All verified.
3. **Researcher.** It searched the web for market size, competitors and regulation, and only kept sources it actually found, so it can't invent links.
4. **Scorer.** It applied our 7-factor scorecard and the valuation formulas.
5. **Dashboard.** A website showing all of this: Portfolio, Year 5, each startup, and a chatbot ("Analyst") you can ask questions.
- **Key line:** "The AI did the reading and checking. We made the decisions." It uses free, open models, so it cost $0.

## Part 5: Q&A, who answers what
- **Money questions (Omar):** concentration, valuation, returns, downside, reserve.
  - Why pay IGNOT's higher price? "Sandbox plus 500 funded investors is rare. We accepted the price but capped the cheque."
  - What if IGNOT and Blaze fail? "That's our downside: 0.35x. The revenue companies and the reserve keep part of the fund."
  - Where do the year-5 numbers come from? "Their own revenue plans, cut down, times published sector multiples."
- **Process, tool and founder questions (Menna):** how did you use AI, can we trust it, why not Hylix.
  - Trust: "Every quote was checked against the source text; every web finding has a link; anything unsourced is labelled ASSUMPTION."
  - Hylix: "Score 3.0 but no revenue and no ask. Watch list."
- **Stuck?** "Our evidence there is thin, which is exactly why that cheque is small."

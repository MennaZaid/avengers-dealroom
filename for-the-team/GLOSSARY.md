# Plain-English guide to the VC words we use

For team members who aren't from business. Each term: what it means, then a small example with our numbers.

## The deal itself

**Round / raise:** a startup sells a slice of its company to investors for cash. *Modeeri is raising a $400K round.*

**Ask:** how much money the startup says it wants in this round.

**Ticket / cheque:** how much *we* put in. It can be less than the full ask; other investors fill the rest. *Our Modeeri ticket is $300K.*

**Pre-seed / seed:** the earliest funding stages. Pre-seed = idea or early product, few or no customers. Seed = a working product with some customers.

**Angel investor:** a wealthy individual investing their own money early. *Modeeri raised $185K from angels.*

**Lead investor:** the investor who sets the terms of a round; others follow. *A Saudi VC already put $110K into Modeeri.*

## What a company is worth

**Valuation:** what the whole company is worth.

**Pre-money valuation:** the company's value *before* the new money comes in.

**Post-money valuation:** pre-money + the new money. *Pre-money $2.38M + $400K round = $2.78M post-money.*

**Equity / stake / ownership %:** the slice of the company we own. **Stake = our cheque ÷ post-money.** *$300K ÷ $2.78M = 10.8% of Modeeri.*

**Scorecard method:** take a typical startup's value in the region and move it up or down depending on how this one compares on team, market, product and so on. Made for pre-seed startups with little revenue.

**Berkus method:** give up to $500K of value for each of five things the startup has de-risked (idea, prototype, team, partnerships, sales). Also made for pre-revenue startups.

**VC method:** estimate what the company could sell for later, then work back to what it's worth today given the risk.

**Multiple (revenue multiple):** a quick way to value a company: yearly revenue × a number. *A software company with $3M revenue at 8x is worth about $24M.*

## Money coming in and going out

**Revenue:** money customers pay the company.

**ARR (Annual Recurring Revenue):** subscription revenue the company expects every year if nothing changes. *Modeeri: $45K+ contracted ARR.*

**Pipeline:** potential deals being discussed but not signed. Worth much less than signed revenue.

**Churn:** customers who stop paying. *Modeeri says 0 churn since March 2026, which is very good.*

**Burn rate:** how much money the company spends per month.

**Runway:** how many months the money lasts. **Runway = cash ÷ monthly burn.** *$400K ÷ $25K a month = 16 months.*

**Milestone:** the goal the money should reach before the company needs to raise again (e.g. "get the FRA licence", "10 paying customers").

**Unit economics:** whether one customer makes or loses money. *Zaat: a founder pays $49 and it costs Zaat $23 to serve them.*

**LTV / CAC:** Lifetime Value (all the money one customer brings over time) ÷ Customer Acquisition Cost (what it costs to win them). Above 3 is considered healthy.

**Gross margin:** the % of revenue left after the direct cost of delivering the product.

**Freemium:** the basic version is free; you pay for extra features.

**Commission / take rate:** the % a marketplace keeps from each transaction. *TRNR takes 10% of each booking.*

## The market

**TAM (Total Addressable Market):** everyone in the world who could ever buy this.

**SAM (Serviceable Addressable Market):** the part of the TAM the startup can actually reach with its product and geography.

**SOM:** the part of the SAM it can realistically win in the next few years.

**B2B / B2C / B2B2C:** selling to businesses / to consumers / to consumers through a business partner (Byota sells through nutritionists).

**SaaS:** Software as a Service: customers pay for software monthly instead of buying it once.

**Moat:** what stops competitors copying you (data, licences, network, brand).

**FRA:** Egypt's Financial Regulatory Authority. It licenses investing and brokerage businesses (matters for Blaze and IGNOT).

## Our fund and its returns

**Portfolio:** all the startups we invest in.

**Allocation / weights:** how we split the $1M. *30% to Modeeri = $300K.*

**Reserve / dry powder:** cash we keep uninvested, for later rounds of our best startups or for surprises.

**Dilution:** every time a startup raises a new round, new shares are created, so our % shrinks. *We own 10.8% of Modeeri today; after two more rounds that each sell 20%, we keep 64% of that, about 6.9%.*

**Exit:** when we get our money back: the company is bought (acquisition) or lists on a stock exchange (IPO).

**Year-5 value:** what our stake is worth in 5 years = our diluted % × the company's value then.

**MOIC (Multiple On Invested Capital):** what the fund is worth ÷ what we put in. *$5.1M ÷ $1M = 5.1x.*

**Base case:** our realistic expectation. **Downside:** what happens if things go badly (some startups fail and are worth $0).

**Return the fund:** one startup alone pays back the whole $1M. VCs look for at least one pick that can do this, because most startups fail.

**Power law:** in VC, a few winners make almost all the returns; most investments return little or nothing.

**Due diligence:** checking a startup's claims before investing. Our Research tab does this.

**Thesis:** our one-sentence rule for what we invest in and why.
